Afternoon — The Himalayas

⛳ The Himalayas Putting Green, St Andrews

Back in St Andrews, we played the famous Himalayas — the Ladies' Putting Club, an 18-hole putting green beside the Old Course, laid out in 1867
Wildly undulating, open to anyone for a few pounds, and enormous fun — putting over hills the size of a house right beside the R&A
The Old Course itself is closed to golf every Sunday by tradition — the links becomes a public park, so the whole town was out walking it
⛳ Round 6 — Dumbarnie Links, 08:50 AM. Then the Himalayas and a lazy afternoon in the Home of Golf.

⛳ THE ST ANDREWS LINKS
"Context for the Home of Golf"

🏔️ Castle Course — ✅ We played it, Fri Jul 3

Character: Dramatic clifftop links overlooking the town and Old Course
Designer: David McLay Kidd (2008) — same architect as Bandon Dunes

🏆 Old Course — 🎲 lost the ballot, walked it instead

Character: The home of golf since 1552 — history on every hole
This trip: We walked the fairways on Saturday the 4th — Swilcan Bridge, the 18th green
Tradition: Closed to golf every Sunday — the links becomes a public park

⛳ The Himalayas — ✅ we played the putting green, Sun Jul 5

Character: The Ladies' Putting Club (1867), an 18-hole green of enormous rolling humps beside the Old Course
Why: Open to all for a few pounds, and more fun than it has any right to be

💪 Jubilee • 📜 New • 🌱 Eden

The other St Andrews Links courses we didn't get to this time — the Jubilee (hardest), the New (Old Tom Morris, 1895), and the Eden (shortest, most forgiving)
Reasons to come back
